
# Daniel G: Closing

Daniel G ("the king of remote sales", Sales Game Tour, trained 2M+ reps, @danielg / @DanielGShow) teaches the close as something set up from the first five seconds of the call. His big claim: **"Fortune is in the amount of attempts on the first call, not in the follow-up."** His tools for it are the **4 Steps** (agenda and exit → current situation → future desire and future pain → game plan and close), the **lamb** posture, **giving the exit so you can bring them back in**, and getting to the **bare naked truth (BNT)** behind an objection in 3 or more attempts. The prospect should never feel those attempts as pressure.

## 1. Role in the Closer Brain

This skill owns **the close**: from the moment discovery is done to "card's through, here's what happens next". That covers:
- the game plan (your pitch, packaged as a plan),
- the ask ("Would you be against…"),
- trial closes and temperature checks earlier in the call,
- the first 3–5 attempts after a stall,
- price framing at the close (Make Money Minimal, price vs cost),
- taking payment and locking the decision in.

Hand off to:
- **Miner**: discovery questioning depth (Daniel G's steps 2–3 overlap; use Miner for question craft, use this skill to check you collected the *closing ammunition*).
- **Hormozi**: offer construction, pricing and guarantees. Daniel G frames the price; Hormozi designs it.
- **Belfort**: tonality and control across the whole call. This skill only covers delivery *at the close*.
- **Voss**: deep objection psychology (labels, accusation audits). Use Daniel G for *staying in the conversation and re-attempting*. Use Voss when an objection needs real unpacking.
- **Owen Santos**: full-call reviews. This skill gives the closing-stage score that goes into that review.

Also check the opening. Daniel G says most closing problems are created there: "we don't have a closing problem, we have an opening problem."

## 2. Core principles (and why they work)

1. **Fortune is in the attempts.** Plan to attempt 3–5+ times on the first call with a qualified buyer. Follow up only after that. *Why:* "Fortune's in the follow-up" programs you to quit at the first objection. And if you follow up on the first (smoke-screen) objection, you're "following up on the wrong objection." His ladder: tour guide (0 attempts, an order-taker), amateur (1), pro (2), master (3+). He calls fewer than three attempts "weak."
2. **The first objection is rarely the truth.** "Never fuel the first objection." Don't pour gasoline on it. Acknowledge it, step back and dig for the BNT. *Why:* If they aren't certain, they won't buy even after payday ("they'll spend it on tequila"). Money, spouse and time objections usually sit on top of a fear: "I don't think I can get your results or your best client's results."
3. **Heart of a lion, sell like a lamb.** Calm, easy, unbothered: "Yeah, don't even worry about it." *Why:* The prospect expects you to argue. When you don't, their shoulders drop and they tell you the truth. "You can stay in a cage with a lamb, not with a lion."
4. **Give the exit to bring them back in.** Make them feel free to leave ("whenever you're ready… you have us in the back of your pocket for the future"). *Why:* A buyer who feels trapped ("a home with no doors") goes defensive and lies. A buyer who feels free answers honestly, and the honest answer is what you close on. "Make the client feel like they're in control. It's the highest form of control."
5. **Urgency, not pressure.** "Pressure is a conflict of internal values; urgency is self-generated." Ask questions that make the buyer produce their own reason to act now (the movie-theatre couple running late). Don't hand them deadlines or fake discounts. *Why:* Self-made urgency survives the hang-up. Pushed urgency turns into "I need to think about it."
6. **Every objection is a mirror.** "No money" means you positioned the offer badly (no anchor or comparison). "Need to think" means you built too little urgency. "Speak to my spouse" means you lacked authority. "You don't hear an objection twice. If you do, you're missing something in the process." *Why:* That shifts your work from rebuttals to call structure.
7. **Certainty transfers.** "People buy certainty. People commit to people more committed than themselves." "Your lack of urgency is their lack of urgency." "Certainty is a transfer of energy, and it's unspoken." Excuses and hesitation lower it. *Why:* Buyers read your energy before your words. "People say yes to you before they say yes to your product."
8. **Questions control the conversation; never prove a prospect wrong.** "I will never prove a prospect wrong and then try to sell them after." Ask "What would you need to see to be certain?" *Why:* People only agree with themselves.
9. **The goal is a decision, not a sale.** (Hormozi said this in their interview and Daniel G agreed: the rep who can say "maybe it's not a fit" saves energy and "feels like a king on the next call.") Use your intuition. After 3+ honest attempts with no movement, stop spending energy on them, close the file with love and protect your state for the next call. Don't sell blamers ("you're just the next bad apple story in their playbook").

## 3. The framework, in call order

### Step 1: Set the agenda and sell the exit (first 60 seconds; this is what makes the close possible)
- Lead like a confident barber: "Here's what we'll do: one, two, three."
- Agenda: "I'll ask you a few questions to see if we can even support you. If we can, I'll show you what we did for people like you. If we can't, I'll point you to someone who can."
- Exit: "…that way, whenever you're ready in the next 6 months to a year, you have us in the back of your pocket for the future."
- **Never** say "and at the end just give me a simple yes or no." It tells them they'll have to decide, which makes them defensive, and then they stop telling you the truth.

### Step 2: Current situation (gather closing ammunition)
What are they doing now, why, for how long, what keeps them there, what do they like and dislike about it, what are the numbers. "I'm gathering intelligence and data… because I use it at the end when I have to give a game plan and close." **Temperature check midway** (the "cake lesson"): "By the way, what's taking you so long to look at something like this?" Let them close themselves before the big close.

### Step 3: Future desire and future pain, then make them confirm
- Desire: "Where do you want to be in 3–6 months? What was the motivation to book this call?"
- Pain (seed it, don't bash): "Have you ever lost a deal because of X? What does that cost you?"
- Make them confirm it with **"Why haven't you already done it?"** Push your own product away: "…not even with us, there are hundreds of options. Why haven't you already fixed this? I just want to better understand." Their answer is the real problem in their own words, and that's what you close on.

### Step 4: Game plan and close
- "Amateurs present. Professionals give a game plan." Ask permission: "Would you be open to me giving you a game plan, like we did for [similar client], over the next 90 days?" Then: "Here's what we're going to do: 1, 2, 3."
- The game plan must include **a shot clock** (90 days, a date), and must **pre-handle money, time and "can I do this"**. Objections at the end mean urgency was missing from the plan.
- **The close ("Would you be against…"):** "Would you be against implementing this game plan to get [their desire, their words] so you don't keep experiencing [their pain, their words], for an investment of $X today?" Tie together the desire from the start, the pain from the middle and the price.
- **Price framing, Triple M (Make Money Minimal):** drop an anchor and a comparison, or the buyer will use their own. Break the price down to a daily figure → compare it to something they already spend money on daily → stack the value → ask "Which gets you closer to [goal]?" Then: "You're already spending the money. We're just reallocating it from things that aren't working to something that will."
- **Take payment immediately:** "Open up your phone, put me on speaker, let me walk you through the process. Simple. Done." Then **lock it in**: future-pace the first 30 days ("when we get you an ROI in the next 30 days…") and give a clear handoff to the next step. In their interview Hormozi added "reinforce the decision": a clear handshake between the sale and the next step prevents cold feet and chargebacks.

### Step 5: If they stall, the attempt loop (3+ times on the same objection)
- **Attempt 1, BNT via the exit:** Use lamb acknowledgement, then give the exit, then offer to send information, then a three-option fear menu. "Hey, 150%, don't even worry about it. Whether it takes four weeks or six, I want you certain. So I can send you something specific, what were you still a little hesitant about: how it works, whether you'd actually use it with your schedule, or how it fits what you already have?" The objection *moves*. Re-enter: "That's my fault, I got so caught up in the investment I forgot to show you how this actually works…" Solve it, then re-close.
- **Attempt 2, justify (intuition check):** "Even when we figure out the capital, how can you justify investing $X right now, in this period of your life or business? I just want to better understand." If they start selling you on why now, ask: "So what do we need to do today to make it happen?" If they can't justify it, don't push.
- **Attempt 3, "If I could… would you…":** Make a give-get trade, never a bare discount. "If I could support you on the investment today, would you be open to doing something for me too: a 2-minute video testimonial after your first 30 days?" Or use **two-word mode**, "What else?", or **price vs cost**: "Price is what you pay today; cost is what you pay ten years down the road."
- **Spouse/partner, third level, back to prospecting:** "What are you two doing tomorrow at 7? Why don't you both jump on a quick five-minute call. I'm not going to sell him. You two can decide that best. I'll just answer his questions." Make the call safe for the spouse.
- **If nothing moves after 3+ honest attempts:** Keep them "in the campfire" (a downsell, a resource, a specific next call tied to the real objection). Never "when do you get paid?" When you follow up, follow up on the BNT, not the smoke screen.

## 4. Word tracks

**Opening the exit (sets up the close)**
1. "Whenever you're ready in the next 6 months to a year, at least you'll have us in the back of your pocket for the future."
2. "My job is just to give you the information. You two go home and make an informed, educated decision."

**Trial closes / temperature checks**
3. "By the way, what's taking you so long to look at something like this?"
4. "Why haven't you already done this, with us or anybody else? I just want to better understand."
5. "Say you don't do anything today and this keeps happening. What's plan B?"

**The ask**
6. "Would you be open to me laying out a game plan like we did for [name] over the next 90 days?"
7. "Would you be against implementing this game plan so you can [desire] and stop [pain], for an investment of $X today?"
8. "Perfect. Open up your phone, put me on speaker, I'll walk you through it." (Daniel G)

**Price**
9. "It's not $1,000, it's $2.70 a day. When's the last time you spent $2.70? … Which gets you closer to [goal]: the coffee, or everything we just went through?"
10. "You're already spending the money. We're just reallocating it from what isn't working to what will."
11. "Is it the price or the cost? Price is what you pay today; cost is what you pay down the road."

**Stall / objection re-entry (lamb mode)**
12. "Yeah, don't even worry about it. We'll figure out ways to raise the capital." (Then give the exit and dig.)
13. "Other than that, what else?"
14. "So I don't send you 18 pages you won't read, what were you still a little hesitant about: A, B or C?"
15. "That's my fault, I got so caught up in the investment I forgot to show you how this actually works."
16. "How can you justify making this investment right now, in this period of your business?"
17. "If I could [concession], would you be open to [give-back] too?"
18. "What would you need to see in order to be certain?" (to "Is this a scam?" / "Does this work?")
19. "Okay." / "150%, I got you." (Acknowledge, then ask a question. Never follow with "but": "the moment you say but, you mess up your acknowledgement.")

**Spouse**
20. "You've been married 18 years, so you two think alike. Since you're the one doing the work, what were *you* still skeptical about?"
21. "Why don't you both jump on a quick 5-minute call tomorrow at 7? I won't sell him. I'll just answer his questions."

**After yes**
22. "Over the next 30 days, here's exactly what we're going to do with you. And when you get that first result, would you be open to giving me a 2-minute video testimonial on the journey?"

**Closing the file (end of follow-up)**
23. "If this isn't a priority right now, totally understand. Just let me know yes or no by 8pm tonight. If I don't hear back I'll close your file. Wishing you the best."

## 5. Tone and delivery

- **Fired up and certain, before the call.** "You get paid for your attitude before you get paid for your skills." "Sell yourself a new frequency before you sell a product." If you believe your product is too expensive, they will too.
- **Lamb voice on objections.** Your voice softens and you smile, almost wave it off: "Yeah, don't even worry about it." Drop *their* shoulders by not raising yours. Do the opposite of the argument they expect.
- **Certain price delivery.** State the investment once, with certainty, then ask the question. Don't apologise, explain or rush to fill the silence. "You will always sell yourself a frequency in your mind before you sell a product." Walk in believing "everybody loves buying expensive things."
- **Lead the dance.** Confident direction, like the barber who doesn't ask permission to move your head. With an over-hyped buyer ("just give me the price"), slow down and run your process anyway. With a defensive buyer, give more exit.
- **Excitement at objections.** "If sales is not exciting when somebody gives you an objection, you're not trained yet." Answer a familiar objection in "two and a half seconds," without hesitation.
- **Speed.** Close when they're hot. His top rep texts "you free for a call right now?" and closes at 2:30 a.m. instead of booking tomorrow. The money is made "in the hidden hours."
- **Non-needy.** "You're good regardless." The client can feel when you need the deal. Talk to enough people that one call never decides your week.

## 6. Mistakes Daniel G calls out (and the fix)

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Quitting after objection #1 and booking a follow-up ("fortune's in the follow-up") | Attempt 3–5 times on the call: BNT, then justify, then if-I-could |
| Arguing ("That's exactly why you need it!") / lion mode | Lamb: "Don't even worry about it," then exit, then dig |
| "When do you get paid?" | It shows you only care about the money and ignores the uncertainty. Find what they were unsure about |
| Saying "but" after agreeing | "Okay, 150%." Then ask a question. Acknowledge; don't negate |
| "Just give me a yes or no at the end" in the opener | Set the agenda and sell the exit |
| Presenting features (a "tour guide") | Package a 90-day game plan with a shot clock |
| Creating pressure (discount if today, it's on sale) | Ask questions that make them produce urgency themselves |
| Letting the buyer compare price to their own anchor | Make Money Minimal, then compare to current spend, then stack value |
| Following up on the smoke-screen objection ("Did you talk to your husband?") | Follow up on the BNT you uncovered |
| Trying to close the spouse | Prospect the spouse onto a "no-sell" 5-minute call |
| Going in for the big close cold (dessert after they're full) | Temperature check midway, "like a turkey," and let them close themselves first |
| Hanging up after the yes with no plan | Lock it in: a 30-day game plan, next steps, future-paced result |
| Needy energy / chasing blamers | Be good regardless. Cut energy on people who blame everyone |

## 7. How to use this skill

**Prep mode** (input: lead info / setter notes). Output:
1. Their likely desire and pain (a hypothesis to confirm in discovery), plus the "why haven't you already?" question to use.
2. A draft 3-point, 90-day game plan with a shot clock, noting where it pre-handles money, time and ability.
3. The "Would you be against…" close written with placeholders for their words.
4. A Make-Money-Minimal breakdown of this offer's price (daily figure plus a likely everyday comparison).
5. The two most likely stalls for this lead, each with a 3-attempt ladder.

**Live / next-line mode** (input: transcript snippet). Output: the **exact line to say next** (one or two sentences, spoken style), then one line on *why* (which step or attempt it is). If they just objected: identify the attempt number, say whether the objection looks like the BNT or a smoke screen, and give the lamb acknowledgement plus the re-entry. Never give more than one option unless asked.

**Call-review mode** (input: full transcript). Find the close (the first game plan or price mention through to the end). Count the attempts. Score with the scorecard below. If the opening created the problem (no exit, "yes or no at the end"), say so.

## 8. Scorecard (closing stage)

Score each 1–5.

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Closing setup** (agenda, exit, ammunition) | No agenda; "yes or no at the end" framing; little data | Agenda but no exit, or thin data | Agenda and exit set; desire, pain and numbers captured in their words |
| **B. Trial closes** | Asks for the sale cold | One temperature check | Temperature checks plus "why haven't you already?" confirmed before pitching |
| **C. Game plan** | Feature dump / tour | Plan without a shot clock, or one that doesn't pre-handle objections | Personalised 1-2-3 plan, timeframe, pre-handles money, time and ability |
| **D. The ask** | No clear ask, or a hedged one ("so… what do you think?") | Clear ask but generic | "Would you be against…" tying their desire and pain to the price; then silence |
| **E. Price framing and delivery** | Apologetic, rushed, no anchor | States price, weak comparison | Certain delivery; anchor and comparison, or Make Money Minimal, or price vs cost |
| **F. Attempts and BNT** | 0–1 attempts; books a follow-up | 2 attempts, or argues | 3+ attempts, lamb tone, exit-then-re-entry, real objection found |
| **G. Payment and lock-in** | No payment taken, or goes robotic | Takes payment, thin next steps | Payment on the call, clear next steps, future-paced win |
| **H. Energy and certainty** | Needy, hesitant, combative | Steady but flat | Fired up, certain, unbothered: "good regardless" |

## A. The 4 Steps to Sell Anything (from "4 Steps to Sell Anything")

His core cadence. Use it to "hold your posture, lead the sale and create urgency, not pressure."

### Step 1: Set the agenda and sell the exit
> "Before I even think about showing you anything, I need to know a few things about you, to see if we can even support you. If we can't, I'll point you to some people who can. If we can, I'll show you what we did for people like you. That way, whenever you're ready, you have us in the back of your pocket for the future."

- **Agenda** = you lead (the barber analogy: "the rougher the barber, the more comfortable you feel… finally somebody knows what they're doing").
- **Exit** = they don't feel trapped. "Come on the call, steal everything, buy nothing." Paradoxically that's what makes them open up.
- **Kill phrase:** "…and then just give me a simple yes or no at the end." It warns the buyer they'll have to decide, so they go defensive from second five.

### Step 2: Current situation
Ask what they use or do now, why, how long, what's kept them there, what they like, what they don't, and the numbers (deal size, revenue, cost of the problem). Don't bash their current situation. You're "visualising the map": where they are (Manhattan), where they want to go (Brooklyn), and the water in between.

### Step 3: Future desire, future pain, confirm
- Desire: "What was the motivation to even look at this? Where do you want to be in 3–6 months?" (Sales is "not just about pain, also desire, want and need.")
- Future pain (seed it as a question): "Have you ever lost, or could you lose, a $50K deal because a buyer formed a perception from [the cheap thing]?"
- **Confirmation question:** "You're at A and want to be at B. Why haven't you already done it?" You can't ask "what are your problems?" This question gets them to say the problems themselves. Push your product away to remove bias: "…and not just with us. There are hundreds of options out there. Why haven't you already made the move? I just want to better understand."

### Step 4: Game plan and close
- "Amateurs present; professionals and world-class people give a game plan."
- Permission: "Would you be open to me giving you a game plan, like we did with [Jill and Sally], who were in your exact position, over the next 90 days, so you can get out of [current]?"
- Deliver it as 1, 2, 3 with a **shot clock**. The plan must knock off:
  - money ("this saves you / returns X"),
  - time ("why now: here's where you should be in 90 days"),
  - skill or ability ("here's how we make sure you can do it").
- "Your lack of urgency is their lack of urgency. People give you objections at the end because there wasn't enough urgency in the presentation."
- **Close:** "Would you be against implementing this game plan to get [desire you told me at the beginning], so you don't experience [pain you told me in the middle], for an investment of [price] today?" He notes you can't use this close unless you collected their current situation, desire, pain and plan.

## B. Urgency vs pressure

- "Pressure is a conflict of internal values. It feels sticky and icky, like a series of yes-questions backing you into a corner." Example: "Buy today, it's on sale."
- "Urgency is asking the right set of questions so the prospect generates the answer themselves." The movie-theatre couple: nobody calls them to say the film starts at 8, yet she jumps out of the car to grab popcorn. The sale happens there because the urgency was self-generated.
- Urgency questions:
  - "Say you don't do this today and this keeps happening. What's plan B?"
  - "If you don't do something about this, what happens to the business in the next 5 years? I just want to better understand."
  - "How fast do you want this handled?"

## C. Price framing at the close

### Triple M: Make Money Minimal
"There always has to be an anchor and a comparison. If you don't drop it, the customer does it in their own head, based on their own bias."
1. Divide the price by the access period down to a daily number ($1,000 / 365 ≈ $2.70/day).
2. "When's the last time you spent $2.70?" Usually it's coffee.
3. Stack the value: list everything they get (training, calls, support, community, the outcome).
4. Contrast: "Which is more valuable / gets you closer to [goal]: the $2.70 coffee every day, or everything we just listed?"
5. Reallocate: "You're already spending the money. We're just moving it from what isn't working to what will."
6. Then: "Open up your phone, put me on speaker, let me walk you through the process."

"You're selling $1,000. I'm selling $2.70 and stacking up the services like they're worth a million dollars. Who's going to say no to that?"

### Price vs cost (Ahmad Mahmood interview)
"Is it the price or the cost? Price is what you pay today. Cost is what you pay down the road." Use the used-car story: you save $20K buying the used BMW, then the electronics, the transmission and the exhaust go, you sell at a loss, and you're out far more than the new one would have cost. "Do you want a cheaper price or a cheaper cost?"

### Comparison and anchor (Sales Bros live demo)
Quantify the problem's annual cost (two lost deals a week × $1K × 52 = $104K) and set the investment against it: "Would it make sense to invest $X to stop losing $104K?" "$1,000 is a lot of money for a phone. Compared to what?"

### "Everybody has money"
"If they tell me they don't have money, they're comparing it to something that's $20 and mine's $500. I suck at positioning."

## D. Objections at the close: the 3-attempt ladders

General rules:
- "A beginner attempts zero or one time. An amateur attempts once. A professional attempts twice. A master goes in three to four times on the same objection."
- "It's the amount of attempts when the prospect doesn't even realise it's an attempt."
- Step 1 of every objection: **get back inside the conversation**, and don't fuel the first objection.
- **Lamb, not lion:** "Great salespeople have the heart of a lion but sell like a lamb."
- "They never said no. They said 'I don't have the money.' Did they say Wells Fargo doesn't have the money?"

### D1. "I don't have the money"
**Attempt 1: lamb, exit, BNT**
> "Yeah, don't even worry about it. We'll figure out ways to raise the capital over the next couple of weeks. In the meantime, even when you do raise it, if you were still a little skeptical I still wouldn't want you to partner with us. So what can I send you so that when you raise it you're certain? Were you still a little skeptical about (1) how it actually works, (2) whether you'd actually use it with the time you have, or (3) how it fits into what you already have? I just want to better understand."
Watch the objection move (e.g. "we bought software two weeks ago and it didn't fit"). Now you have the real fear (being burned again). Solve it, then re-close: "So, ready to get started so we can take care of [problem] and move toward [benefit]?"

**Attempt 2: justify**
> "Totally understand. Eventually you'll have the funds. Let me ask, though: how can you justify investing [price] right now, in this period of your life or business? I just want to better understand."
"The harder the questions you ask, the easier it is to close." If they sell you on why now ("we've been losing so much money…"), ask: "So what are we going to do to raise the capital today, so we can take care of this tomorrow?" If they can't justify it, use your intuition and don't push.

**Attempt 3: "If I could… would you…"**
> "If I could support you on the investment today, say half now and half in 30 days, would you be open to doing something for me as well? When we get you a return in the next 30 days, would you give me a 2–3 minute video testimonial on the journey?"
This is a give-get trade, not a free discount. The testimonial ask also signals certainty ("people commit to people more committed than themselves"). No discount available? Downsell something to keep them "in the campfire."

**Never:** "So when do you get paid?" It "shows you don't care about me anymore," and if they're uncertain they'll spend Friday's pay elsewhere anyway.

### D2. "I need to talk to my spouse / partner"
**Level 1: two-word mode.** Lamb: "Yeah, totally, don't even worry about it… other than that, what else?" The objection often moves ("…it's too expensive").

**Level 2: honour the marriage, isolate their own doubt.**
> "What's his name? How long have you two been married? Eighteen years. Your marriage has lasted longer than most businesses. You obviously think alike. Let's say he's supportive and says 'babe, I love this.' Since you said you'll be the one doing this, what were *you* still a little skeptical about, so I can put it in an email for you both? Was it the return on investment, the time it takes, or the price?"
"Where did Rodrigo go? He disappeared." Then you're on the real objection.

**Level 3: back to prospecting (get the spouse on a call).**
> "What are you two doing tomorrow at 7? I have an idea. Why don't you and [name] jump on a quick 5-minute call? I'll show him exactly what I showed you and answer any questions. I'm not going to sell him. I think you can do that best. Then you two go home and make an informed, educated decision together."
"Jump on / hop on / pop on." Never say "meeting." Give the spouse the exit, because "nobody brings their spouse on a call if they feel the spouse is walking into a trap." Don't emasculate the buyer. They're still the decision-maker.

Mirror: "If they need to speak to the spouse, you lacked authority in the sale."

### D3. "Send me some information / I need to think about it / I don't make decisions this fast"
> "150%. I want you to make an informed, educated decision, whether that takes 4 weeks or 6. When you join I want you to be a [hell] yes. What's your best email? And a number to text it to?… Since you're busy, I'm not going to send you 18 pages. I'll send something specific. After everything we went over, what were you still a little hesitant or confused about, as to why you wouldn't start today? Was it the payment and ROI, whether the product fits your lifestyle, or how you'd actually use it?"
They name it ("the payment plan"). Re-enter: "That's my fault. I got so caught up in talking about the investment I forgot to walk you through how this actually works…" Then go back into the presentation, then re-close.
Mirror: "If they need time to think, you lacked urgency."

### D4. "Not interested" (brush-off)
Don't fight it ("But what are you not interested in?" turns into an argument). Acknowledge, exit, then ask a question.

### D5. "Is this a scam? / Does this actually work?"
> "That's a really interesting perspective. Whether it's with us or anyone else, what would you need to see in order to be certain?"
"I will never prove a prospect wrong and then try to sell them after. Questions control the conversation."

### D6. When to stop
After 3+ honest attempts with no movement, use your intuition ("a faculty God gave you"). "I don't think this conversation is going to close. Let me stop losing my energy." Be good regardless. Don't sell people who blame every company for their failures. Keep them in the ecosystem if they're a real fit.

## E. Buyer states (read them before you close)

- **Defensive** (about 95% of buyers): "I want the thing, I just don't want to buy it from a salesperson." Fix: more exit, lamb tone, drop their shoulders. "You turned an extrovert into an introvert by your language at the beginning."
- **Hyped / over-embellished:** "Just show me the price." They try to rush you past qualifying, and that's how you get ghosted. Fix: lead the dance and run your process anyway.
- **Ready:** go to the game plan and close.
- Buyer's goal: "get everything, buy nothing." Your goal: qualify, then sell. "Sales is a dance, and you lead."

---

## F. Temperature checks and trial closes

- **The cake lesson:** Don't offer dessert after they're full. Ask mid-meal what they're in the mood for. Likewise, ask midway: "What's taking you so long to look at something like this?" and "Why haven't you already done it?" "Let them close themselves before you go in for the big close. That's what great closers do. They temperature check and test close."
- In the Hormozi interview, both agreed you shouldn't attempt the close until you know it's there. If you sense it isn't, ask "What else are you concerned about?"

## G. Mindset for closers (from reels and interviews)

- **Ask.** "The person that asks the most is the person that makes the most. Right around the corner was a yes, but the guy stopped asking."
- **Certainty.** "Certainty is a transfer of energy, and it's unspoken. Excuses lower your certainty." (The pay-the-bill-before-it-hits-the-table reel.)
- **Attitude.** "You get paid for your attitude before you get paid for your skills. People buy the face, they don't buy the business."
- **Frequency.** "You will always sell yourself a frequency in your mind before you sell a product in person." The rep who wakes up thinking "my product's too expensive" sells less than the one who thinks "everybody loves buying expensive things."
- **People are spending.** They buy the 3 S's: Speed, Solution, Status. "The only question is, are they going to spend it with you?"
- **Sell the story and meaning before the product.** "Nobody cares about the product. They only care about what it can do for them, and the way it makes them feel."
- **Close yourself first.** "You'll close to the degree that you're closed." The first sale is you.
- **No ghosting problem, a pipeline problem.** "You don't have a closing problem, you have a not-enough-people problem." Volume removes neediness.
- **Mental shin pads.** "All the money is made on the third and fourth attempt." When the door's closing, put your foot back in: "One second, so I don't get fired today, what's the number one reason you didn't feel comfortable?"
- **Momentum trap.** When your close rate rises, don't cut your volume. Double down.
- **Sales is the only job** "where you can shut your mouth, go close a client, and make them pay the bill."

## H. Follow-up (only after the attempts)

- "Fortune is only in the follow-up when fortune is not in the close on the first call."
- Follow up on the **real** objection you uncovered, not the smoke screen ("Did you talk to your husband?" is following up on the wrong objection).
- Remind them why they booked. "Nobody remembers why they talked to you."
- Closing-the-file message:
  > "If this isn't on your priority list right now, I completely understand. Just let me know yes or no by 8pm Eastern tonight. If I don't hear back I'll close your file, since we're accepting a couple of other clients. I wish you the best."
- Same-day speed: "You free for a call right now?" beats booking tomorrow.

## I. Quick diagnostic: objection → what you did wrong earlier

| Objection at the close | Root cause (his mirror) | Fix earlier in the call |
|---|---|---|
| "I don't have the money" | Bad positioning: no anchor or comparison | Quantify the pain's cost; Make Money Minimal; price vs cost |
| "I need to think about it" | Not enough urgency | Shot clock in the game plan; "why haven't you already?" |
| "I need to talk to my spouse" | Lacked authority | Lead the dance; confirm they're the decision maker in the opener |
| "Send me info" | Buyer felt trapped, so there's a hidden fear | Sell the exit in step 1; dig with the fear menu |
| Quiet, one-word answers | Opening raised their defences | Agenda and exit; lamb tone; no "yes or no" framing |
