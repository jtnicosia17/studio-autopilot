
# Belfort: tone and control (Straight Line System)

## 1. Role in the Closer Brain

This skill covers **how the closer sounds and who controls the call**. That runs from the first 4 seconds to the final "sound fair enough?". It is the layer that sits under every other stage. Belfort's claim is that a script is about 10% of the game. The other 90% is tonality and body language, and they decide whether the words land at all.

Own these moments:
- **The open (first 4 seconds).** Set the frame: sharp, enthusiastic, expert.
- **Keeping control.** Stay inside the line's boundaries and pull back prospects who drift "off to Pluto".
- **Tonality of every question and transition.** Rapport gets built while you gather information, not in a separate chit-chat phase.
- **The certainty arc.** Raise the three tens (product, you, company) and use looping tonality (enter their world, then pace and lead).
- **Closing tonality.** Absolute certainty, then utter sincerity, then the reasonable man.

Hand off to the other skills for the content:
- **Miner**: what to ask in discovery. Belfort covers how you ask it and the order of control.
- **Hormozi**: the offer, value stack and price.
- **Daniel G**: close mechanics and payment flow.
- **Voss**: deep objection handling and labels. Belfort covers deflect, then loop, then re-ask.
- **Owen Santos**: full-call review structure. This skill provides the tone and control sub-score.

## 2. Core principles (and why they work)

1. **Every sale is the same.** Belfort first said it in 1988 and drew it on a whiteboard as a straight line. The open is on the left, the close on the right, and there are boundaries above and below. Why it works: if the order is fixed, the rep can stop improvising and put their attention on the prospect and on their own tone.
2. **Sales is the transference of certainty, which is an emotion.** You are at a 10 and they are lower, and your job is to lift them. There are two kinds of certainty:
   - **Logical certainty** comes from the words, which build an "airtight logical case".
   - **Emotional certainty** comes from tonality and body language. Belfort calls these "the hidden language of influence" because they get past the critical mind and work on the gut, where decisions are made.

   People buy on emotion and justify with logic. Logic alone always "wants to think about it".
3. **Control goes to the expert.** We are trained from childhood to defer to experts (the family doctor asks and we answer). If you are perceived as an expert, the prospect hands you control: they answer your questions and let you guide. If you sound like a novice, they take control, answer questions with questions, and the call spirals.
4. **Control does not mean talking.** Control means you ask smart questions in a set order and the prospect does most of the talking. Belfort's view is that, left alone, reps talk too long.
5. **Tonality makes them listen instead of narrate.** In a flat voice, the listener runs their own commentary in their head. When your voice has tone, they lock onto it and try to work out what you mean, and that gives you control.
6. **Rapport rests on two feelings: "you care about me" and "you're like me".** You build it with tonality and active listening, not by faking common interests. ("Oh my god, you like fishing? I love fishing!" is how reps go off the line.)
7. **Charisma is learnable.** It has three parts: effective tonality, appropriate body language, and "not saying stupid shit".
8. **The truth well told.** There are 50 ways to describe yourself or the product. Find the one that is true and also sounds amazing. Most damage comes from reps who lie when the truth would do, or who run out of smart things to say.
9. **Be dynamic, not wooden.** The line is the average path, and the boundaries give you room to move. Know your script cold so you can focus on tone and on the prospect, but never sound like you are reading it, because that kills every sale.

## 3. The framework, in call order

Belfort's **Straight Line syntax** has 12 steps. This skill drives tone and control at every step.

| # | Step | Tone and control job |
|---|------|---------------------|
| 1 | **Take immediate control (first 4 seconds)** | Come across as **sharp as a tack, enthusiastic as hell, an expert in your field**. On the phone you have 4 seconds; in person, about a quarter of a second. |
| 2 | **Gather intelligence** | Ask questions in a planned order, using "I care" and "I really want to know" tonality. Sound like the trusted family physician, not the "grand inquisitor". |
| 3 | **Build rapport (at the same time as step 2)** | Active listening: "uh-huh", "got it", "wow". Lean in on emotion, lean back on logic. Mirror their pace. |
| 4 | **Transition** | Say it with certainty: "Based on what you've told me, this is a perfect fit for you. Let me tell you why." |
| 5 | **Straight Line presentation** | Lead with benefits, not features. Future-pace what life looks like after. Don't front-load: ask for the order about a third of the way down the line and hold material back for the loops. |
| 6 | **Ask for the order (first time)** | Use the tonality chain: absolute certainty, then utter sincerity, then the reasonable man ("sound fair enough?"). |
| 7 | **First objection: deflect** | Don't answer it head-on. "I hear what you're saying, but let me ask you a question: does the idea make sense to you? Do you like the idea?" Ask it in the money-aside hypothetical tone. |
| 8 | **Loop** | Read their certainty from how they answer, enter their world, pace and lead, re-present one ten, then ask for the order again. |
| 9 | **Lower the action threshold** | Run the "worst that can happen" against the upside. Shrink the negative movie in their head and enlarge the positive one. |
| 10 | **Raise the pain threshold** | Bring back the pain you uncovered in step 2. Hold it; don't relieve it too early. |
| 11 | **More loops if needed** | Each loop raises one of the tens and ends with a close. |
| 12 | **Customers for life** | Follow through, then get referrals. |

**The three tens.** A prospect buys only when they are close to a 10 on each of these:
1. The **product**: they love it, not just like it.
2. **You**: they trust you and feel you care.
3. **Your company**: they trust it will deliver.

"Let me think about it" means one of the tens is missing. Work out which one and raise it.

**Reading certainty from the deflect answer:**
- "It's amazing" is roughly a 9. Move to the next ten.
- "Sounds pretty good" is about a 6.5 to 7. You have work to do on this ten.
- "It sounds okay" is a 5.5 to 6.
- "It's all right" is a 4 to 5.

**Enter their world, then pace and lead.** If they say "sounds pretty good" (about a 7), answer at a 7.1: "Exactly, it really is a great idea." Don't jump to a 10, or they will feel unheard. Then ratchet your certainty up sentence by sentence through the re-presentation ("…and when you take that together with…") until you finish at a 10. Your words move them logically; your rising tone moves them emotionally.

**Keeping them on the line.** When the prospect drifts off the line (rambling, story time, a counter-question, an attempt to take over):
1. Acknowledge them briefly, in a warm tone: "Ah, yup, totally."
2. Agree with them.
3. Bridge back with your next question: "So let me ask you…"

Never follow them off to "Pluto". Never answer a question with a long monologue. If you can't ask your next question, you've lost control.

## 4. Word tracks

**Open (first 4 seconds)**
1. Lead with *their* world, not your name or company. For example: "Hey John, [relevant thing in their market]. 60 seconds is all I need." (Belfort's example: a competitor just opened two new locations.)
2. "How you doing today?" asked as if you genuinely want the answer, not as a perfunctory greeting.
3. Ask permission in the reasonable-man tone: "If you've got 60 seconds, I want to share an idea with you. Got a minute?"
4. The "no big deal" frame: "I'm not looking to sell you anything today, no big deal. We'll just see if it even makes sense."
5. The scarcity drop, lowering your voice: "Listen, John… the reason for the call today…"

**Discovery and control**
6. Set the agenda as the expert, for example: "Let me ask you a few questions so I can see whether I can actually help you. If I can't, I'll tell you."
7. Ask about pain in the "I care" tone: "So what's *really* keeping you up at night right now?"
8. Bring a drifting prospect back to the line: "Ha, love that. And so let me ask you…"
9. Instead of "I hear what you're saying," say: **"I totally understand what you're saying, John, but let me ask you a question…"** Belfort's point is that the first sounds skeptical, as if you're waiting for your turn, and the second sounds as if you're on their side.

**Transition and presentation**
10. "Based on everything you've told me, this is a perfect fit for you, and let me tell you why."
11. Sell the benefit, not the feature: "Imagine how you're feeling six months from now…"
12. Future-pace: "The only problem you'll have is wishing you'd started six months ago."

**Closing tonality chain**
13. Absolute certainty: "Give me one shot…" Then utter sincerity: "…and believe me, you will not be sorry." Then the reasonable man: "Sound fair enough?"

**Deflecting and looping**
14. "I hear what you're saying, but let me ask you a question. Money aside, does the idea make sense to you? Do you like the idea?"
15. "Exactly. It really is a great idea. In fact, the true beauty of it is…" This loops you back into presentation mode, picking up where you left off.
16. "Now do you see what I'm saying? Does that make sense to you?" Then answer "Exactly."
17. Re-introduce yourself for the second ten: "Let me reintroduce myself a little bit and tell you what I stand for…"
18. Lower the action threshold: "What's the worst that can happen? Let's say I'm wrong… is that going to put you in the poorhouse? But on the upside, if I'm even half as good as I say I am…"
19. Start small on the trust ten: "I'm not asking to get married right now. I'm asking for one shot."

**Price ("too expensive")**
20. "You're right, it's premium." Agree, then fork: is it value (loop and build certainty) or budget (a different conversation)? Never defend the price.

**Word swaps** (from his never-say lists)
- Say "premium", not "expensive".
- Say "affordable", not "cheap".
- Say "explore", not "try".
- Say "Got a quick idea for you", not "just checking in".
- Say "Does Tuesday or Thursday work?", not "let me know if you're interested".
- Say "Who else should we bring in?", not "Are you the decision maker?"
- Drop "to be honest", "sorry to bother you" and "trust me" altogether, and show proof instead.

## 5. Tone and delivery

**The tonalities.** These are the core influencing tones. Belfort's point is that you already use all of them; the skill is using them on purpose, at the right moment.

| Tonality | When to use it | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute certainty** | Key claims, the start of the close | Staccato and fully enunciated. It can even be a whisper. |
| **Utter sincerity** | Right after a certainty claim, during the close | Smooth and soft, no pressure. Unspoken: "I have your best interest at heart." |
| **Reasonable man** | End of the close, asking for time ("got a minute?") | Upward lilt with a slight shrug: "sound fair enough?" Unspoken: "only an unreasonable person would say no." |
| **Scarcity / secret** | The reason for the call, insider points | Lower the voice: "Listen… the reason for the call today…" |
| **"I care" / "I really want to know"** | Greetings and pain questions | Warm and curious, as if you actually want the answer. |
| **Money-aside hypothetical** | Deflecting the first objection | Light and casual: "money aside… does it make sense to you?" |
| **Declarative as a question** | Introductions | A statement with the voice raised at the end, implying familiarity. Belfort gave a radio host's "Hey Jordan, this is Sean from Pelco Fuel" as a model. |
| **"No big deal" / bottled enthusiasm** | Throughout | Energy kept just below the surface, "like a volcano about to erupt" but never unleashed. Not the Tony Little hype voice. |

Other tones from his book that are worth knowing (they are background, not from the scraped set): **mystery and intrigue** and **implied obviousness**.

**Rules for using them:**
- **Mix them.** If you stay in one tone too long, the prospect gets used to it and tunes out.
- **Pace first, then lead.** Match their energy and certainty, then raise yours.
- **Ask for the order in the chain:** certainty, then sincerity, then the reasonable man. Belfort's rule is to never *close* in absolute certainty. The final "sound fair enough?" is the reasonable man.
- **Use a script so you can listen.** Knowing your words lets you focus on your own tone and on what their tone reveals about their certainty.
- **Ask calmly and casually**, "like you've done it a thousand times", and close on the call. Certainty has a half-life; a payment link sent later gives them time to run "the negative movie".

**Body language and voice** (on Zoom, apply the camera equivalents):
- Give active-listening feedback ("uh-huh", "got it", "wow") while they talk. Going silent with a blank stare and then jumping to the next question loses them.
- Lean in when they talk about emotional things (family, pain); lean back for logic (numbers).
- Don't freeze like a robot. Rookies who sit still can't close.
- In person: angle off with men (face-on reads as confrontational); go face-to-face with women but respect their personal space. Never crowd someone.

## 6. Mistakes Belfort calls out

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Opening with "Hi, I'm X from Y company, how are you today?" They hear "salesman". | Open with their world and something they'd want to know. Sound like an expert with information. |
| Hype enthusiasm (the Tony Little voice) | Bottled enthusiasm: intensity below the surface. |
| A perfunctory "how you doing?" | Ask it as if you mean it. |
| Interrogating ("grand inquisitor" questions in a flat tone) | Trusted-physician tone, plus active listening. |
| Faking rapport ("I love fishing too!") and drifting off the line | Rapport comes from "you care" and "you're like me". Acknowledge, then bridge back. |
| Taking control in order to talk, and talking too long | Control means asking questions. Let them talk. |
| Front-loading the whole pitch before the first ask | Ask for the order about a third of the way down the line and hold material back for the loops. |
| Selling features ("150 machines, a sauna…") | Sell benefits and future-pace them. |
| Answering "let me think about it" head-on with a rebuttal | It's a smokescreen for uncertainty. Deflect, find the missing ten, loop, re-ask. Otherwise they jump to the next objection ("let me talk to my wife"). |
| Coming in at a 10 when they're at a 6 ("EXACTLY!") | Enter their world at 0.1 above them, then ratchet up. |
| Defending the price | Agree it's premium, fork value versus budget, loop. |
| Staying in one tone, or sounding wooden or read from a script | Mix the tonalities; know the script so well it disappears. |
| Sending a payment link or closing "later" | Close on the call while certainty is at its peak. |
| Weasel words: "to be honest", "trust me", "try", "sorry to bother you" | Use the word swaps in section 4. |
| Lying when the truth would do | Tell the truth well. Ethics are non-negotiable (Belfort went to prison; he purged anything manipulative from the current system). |

## 7. How to use this skill

**Prep mode.** Given lead notes, produce:
1. A 4-second opener: their world plus a reason to listen.
2. The control setup line for the start of discovery.
3. Three or four discovery questions, each with its tonality marked.
4. A prediction of which of the three tens is likely to be weakest, with a planned loop for it.
5. The closing tonality line.
6. One drift risk and the bridge-back line for it.

Keep it to one screen.

**Live mode (next line).** Given a transcript snippet:
1. Say where they are on the line: open, discovery, transition, presentation, first ask, or a loop.
2. Say who is in control.
3. If there's an objection, estimate their certainty (1 to 10) and name the missing ten.
4. Give the **exact next line**, with a tonality tag in brackets, for example: `[reasonable man] Sound fair enough?`
5. Explain why in one sentence.

If they're off the line, the next line is an acknowledgment plus a bridge question, not an answer.

**Call review mode.** Given a full transcript:
1. Score the scorecard below and quote the timestamped moments.
2. Rewrite the worst moment.
3. Assign one drill.

Text transcripts hide tone, so infer it from the word choice, hedges, filler, who speaks how much, interruptions, and how the prospect responded. Say when a score is inferred.

## A. The model: why tone = control

### A1. Every sale is the same (the Straight Line)
- Draw a line: OPEN (left) → CLOSE (right). The perfect sale walks the line: every statement gets "yes, sounds great". Rare.
- Boundaries above and below = how far the conversation can wander while you're still in control. Outside = "off to Pluto" (or "down to your anus" in Belfort's crude phrasing). [Y5, Y2]
- The boundaries are also your **wiggle room**: real sales zig-zag (you may present a bit while still asking questions; fill things in out of order). The line is "the most average sale", not a rigid script. Rigid/wooden = "the death knell of every sale". [Y5]
- Syntax = order. "If you put the butter in before... you're screwed." Control → intelligence → rapport (simultaneous) → transition → presentation → ask → deflect → loop → re-ask → lower action threshold → raise pain threshold → more loops → customers for life. [Y2]

### A2. Certainty is the product you're actually selling
- Sales = transference of emotion; specifically the emotion of certainty. You at 10 → them up the scale until they cross their buying threshold. [Y4, S08]
- **Logical certainty**: P&L makes sense, cost–benefit adds up, better than alternatives. Built by WORDS (airtight logical case).
- **Emotional certainty**: gut feeling "I must have this". Built by TONALITY + BODY LANGUAGE + future pacing. [Y4]
- Logic alone triggers "let me think about it / let me call you back" — "the logical mind always wants to know more… it's not where decisions get made." [Y4]
- "A real closer can sell with just average words… stop collecting bogus scripts and start training yourself to sound like an expert." [S08]
- Communication split he teaches: ~45% tonality, ~45% body language, ~10% words (on the phone tonality carries ~80–90%). He hedges "different numbers by study". [Y1, P4]

### A3. Why the first 4 seconds decide control
- Humans judge books by covers ("our parents told us not to… but they did, and so do we"). Fear-based, reptilian snap judgment. [Y3, P4]
- In 4 seconds (phone) / ¼ second (in person) you must be perceived as:
  1. **Sharp as a tack** — on the ball, a born problem solver, won't waste their time.
  2. **Enthusiastic as hell** — *bottled* enthusiasm: "sits below the surface like a seething volcano… I don't unleash it, but I could." NOT "Oh my god Bill, you can't live without this."
  3. **An expert in your field** — the most important. [Y1, Y3, X6]
- These "chunk up" to: *"this is a person worth listening to — they might help me get what I want."* [Y3]
- Expert → prospect **defers**: answers your questions, lets you guide discovery. Novice → prospect "intuitively tries to take control", answers questions with questions, sale spirals. [Y3]
- No words can do this in 4 seconds ("Hey, I'm an expert" → "what's wrong with this guy?"). It's tonality and body language. [Y1, P4]
- Belfort's origin story: his young brokers couldn't close rich people; he realised he had "a certain way of speaking that allowed me to immediately take control of the conversation… at the heart of all influence." [Y1]

### A4. Control ≠ talking
- "When you're in control you're asking very smart questions and it's the prospect that's doing the talking." [Y1]
- "One of the biggest mistakes… when I say take immediate control it's not so you can talk talk talk." [Y2]
- To Grant Cardone: "in the absence of intervention they'll talk too long" — install procedures so reps know when to stop. [P3]
- Tonality makes them hook into you: monotone → they "narrate against you" in their head; with tonality they "zero in… what does he really mean… it gives me control." [Y1]

## B. Openers (first 4 seconds) — situations

**Cold/semi-warm outbound**
- Bad: "Hi, I'm Jordan from XYZ Corporation, how are you today?" → they hear "salesman". [S01]
- Good: lead with *their* world + useful info: "Hey John, [competitor] just opened two new locations in your market. 60 seconds is all I need." "One opening begs for time; the other brings value in the first breath." [S01]
- Use first names ("Hey, is Jordan there?") — more personable than "Mr. Belfort", gets past gatekeepers. [P4]
- Declarative-as-question: say your company with a lift ("This is Sean from Pelco Fuel↗") so they wonder "do I know them?" and start listening. Only ~1 in 100 do this naturally. [P4]
- Permission in reasonable-man tone: "If you have 60 seconds I want to share an idea with you. Got a minute?" [P4]
- "No big deal" frame: "I don't want to sell you anything, I don't need your credit card… we'll be in your area next week, just love to chop it up." [P4]
- Personal touch/humor: "I hear a baby in the background — I'm sure you're saving for college." [P4]
- Scarcity drop: lower your voice: "Listen John… the reason for the call today…" → "what?" [P4]
- "Now he sounds like a salesman" moment = pitching the deal ("I can save you $200 a year on heating fuel") before earning the right. Tell a bit more story first. [P4]

**Booked call / inbound (closer context — adaptation)**
- First line should still carry sharp + enthusiastic + expert: warm greeting with genuine "how you doing today?" (mean it), then immediately set the frame: "I've got about [X] minutes — what I'd like to do is ask you a few questions, understand where you're at and where you want to be, and if I can help I'll show you how; if not I'll tell you. Fair enough?" (reasonable man).
- Selling Live open: "the way I usually work, I want to know about you, your company, what's right in your business, what's wrong… based on that we tailor-make…" then one-sentence credibility (three areas of excellence) then straight into "if you had to say one thing that's giving you the most problem, what would it be?" [P1]

**"How you doing today?"**
- Perfunctory version = "I know that you know that I know I don't care." Tonality version = they feel "he really wants to know." Two reps with the same script: one gets 30 leads/day, the other 8–10 — the difference is tonality. [X6, X11]

## C. Discovery with control (intelligence gathering + rapport)

- Rapport is built *simultaneously* with intelligence gathering via the tone of the questions and your listening — not as a separate small-talk phase. [Y2]
- Two ways to ask the same question: "grand inquisitor" ("so how long you been doing that, where do you live at") → they clam up; or "trusted family physician/advisor" → they open up. [Y3, Y2]
- What to find: pain points, needs, values, financial qualification. If you can't ask, "you can't close anybody." [Y2]
- Pain question tone: flat "what's your biggest pain point?" → "f--- you". Caring "So what's your biggest pain point right now? Like what's *really* keeping you up at night?" → "oh my God, he cares, he feels my pain." [S10]
- Active listening: give energy back — "uh-huh, mmm, ooh, oh my god, got it, yup, oh nice, wow very cool". Blank stare + "Got it. Okay. Now where did you grow up?" = losing. [S10, P4]
- Body: lean in for emotion (family, pain), lean back for logic. Rookies sit still "like a robot" — "can't close a sale." [P4]
- Don't fake commonality ("I'm a fisherman too" when you're not). The "fishing tangent" is the classic drift-off-the-line. [Y5, P4]
- Pen principle: never pitch first — "How could you possibly sell anything to anyone without first asking them questions?" (How long in the market? What do you use now? What didn't it do? If you found the right one, what next?) [S02, IG caption]
- Selling Live discovery pattern [P1]: open question on biggest problem → follow the thread ("you have nothing there right now at all?") → insert expertise ("I was doing research on the industry… you're really scratching the surface domestically") → role/decision mapping ("where do you fit in… do you have a CFO?") → restate their challenge better than they did ("how do we get there in a way that's risk-averse but lets us test the waters…") → "you're right" from the prospect = rapport + authority.

**Keeping them on the line**
- When they spiral: "listen, say ah, yup, um, agree with them… and then bring them right back and move down the straight line." [Y3]
- When a prospect asks a question instead of answering: short answer, then your question. Answering at length = they now control.
- Don't follow them off into tangents to "build rapport" — "that's just repulsive" (shows you're a novice). [Y5]

## D. Transition and presentation

- Transition line: "Jim, based on what you said to me, this is a perfect fit for you — let me tell you why." [Y2, P4]
- Presentation = features AND benefits, "most importantly" benefits, tied to the problems uncovered. Gym example: features put them to sleep; "imagine how great you're feeling six months from now" closes. [Y2, S07]
- Future pacing (words + tonality) creates emotional certainty: they imagine themselves using it, getting the result, pain gone. [Y4]
- Don't front-load: ask for the order the first time ~⅓ down the line; "it's not like you want to make your presentation so long… by the time you're done there's nothing left for you to say." [X7]
- Selling Live "start small" frame: "I could best serve you by just starting off doing one thing for you really, really well… give me 30 days… if I'm even half as good as I say, you'll be moving all your business over… that's not fair enough?" [P1]
- Truth well told: "There's many ways to describe your product; only one is the actual best way — interesting, logical, cool and honest." [X6]

## E. Closing tonality

**The chain** (Belfort calls this his most reliable pattern for "people on the fence, teetering"):
1. **Absolute certainty** — staccato, enunciated, "forget the words, it's just so sure". Can be a whisper.
2. **Utter sincerity** — "collapses" into smooth/soft: "and believe me, John, the only problem you'll have is that you didn't get started six months ago" (unspoken: no pressure, I have your best interest at heart).
3. **Reasonable man** — "sound fair enough?" (unspoken: if you say no, you're unreasonable).
- Wave shape: intensity peaks in the logic/certainty, dips into sincerity, lifts on "fair enough?". "You never want to close in the tone of absolute certainty." [Y4, P4]
- Breakfast Club version: "You give me one shot [certainty] — and believe me [sincerity] you will not be sorry — sound fair enough? [reasonable man]" [P4]

**Close on the call**
- "Never send your client a payment link… they start running the negative movie… let me think about it. You had the sale and you mailed it away." Ask "calm as can be… casual, no big deal, like you've done it a thousand times." Confirm details, stay on the line, finish together. "Certainty has a half-life." [S09]
- Assumptive next step: "Let's open up the account now." [P1]

## F. Back half: deflect → loop → re-ask

### F1. Objections are smokescreens for uncertainty
- Three outcomes to the first ask: yes (laydown, rare), no (rare if you qualified), maybe = common objections ("let me think about it", "let me call you back", "bad time of year", "talk to my wife"). [Y2, X7, P4]
- Your income = your ability to turn the *common objections* into buyers; "any lousy salesperson can close the laydowns." [X7, X12]
- Rebutting head-on ("smart people don't think about it, blah blah") accomplishes nothing — they "hop to a new objection… let me talk to my wife… every single time." [X7]

### F2. Deflect (step 1 of every loop)
- "I hear what you're saying, Jim, but let me ask you a question — almost hypothetically speaking, money aside — does the idea make sense to you? Do you like the idea?" [X7, Y2]
- Upgrade: "I *totally understand* what you're saying, John, but let me ask you a question" (the first sounds skeptical and waiting your turn; the second says I'm on your side). [IG caption, Jul 2026]
- Deflection keeps rapport ("we're on the same page still") and *measures* certainty.

### F3. Read the certainty scale from the answer (product ten)
| They say | Approx. | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| "Oh yeah, it's amazing" | 9+ | "Exactly." → move to ten #2 or #3 — something else is holding them back |
| "Sounds pretty good" | 6.5–7 | Enter at 7.1 → loop product ten |
| "Sounds okay" | 5.5–6 | Loop product ten harder |
| "It's all right" | 4–5 | Real work; consider re-discovery |
[X7]

### F4. Enter their world + pace and lead
- Don't blast "EXACTLY!" at a 7 — "whoa, I don't think he heard me." Match +0.1. [X7]
- Then: "Exactly. It really is a great idea. In fact, the true beauty of it is it also has ABC… and DEF… and when you take ABC and DEF together…" — certainty ratchets up line by line. End: "Now Jim, you see what I'm saying? Does that make sense?" → "Oh yeah." → "Exactly." Moves 6 → 9. [X7]
- Secondary presentation = plumbing/electrics/paint on the house framed by the first presentation; together they form "the ultimate scream-from-the-hilltops airtight logical case." [X7, X12]
- Don't ask for the order after only raising the product ten — "three tens need to move, not one." [X7]

### F5. Ten #2 (you) and ten #3 (company) — from Selling Live [P1]
- Hypothetical relationship reframe: "Honestly, if I'd been your broker for the last three or four years making you money consistently, you wouldn't be saying let me think about it — you'd say just take over, am I right?" → "Of course."
- Reintroduce yourself: "Let me reintroduce myself a little bit — what I stand for… I put my clients' needs above everything… I'll hold your hand every step of the way."
- Company ten: tenure, track record, "we didn't get where we are by burning our clients."
- Address "who else will I deal with?": "You'll only be dealing with me."

### F6. Lower the action threshold (Belfort's "magic bullet")
- Everyone has a preset certainty level needed to buy. Belfort: low threshold ("I'm a sucker… you could sell me the Brooklyn Bridge" — buys at 7). His father: needs a 10 "and damn sure of it". [Y2, P4]
- Fulcrum idea (IG caption): same 10 pros/10 cons → different decisions depending on where their beliefs put the balance point. Sell to how they weigh the facts.
- Mechanism: before buying people run two movies — positive (benefits) and negative (what if it goes wrong). High-threshold people run a long negative movie and short positive one. Use language to flip it: "What's the worst that can happen? Let's say I'm wrong… there's a guarantee… is that going to destroy your life? But on the upside, if this is even half as good as I say…" then list benefits → ask again. [P4, P1]
- Selling Live version: "Let's say I'm wrong… you lose one basis point… is that going to put you in the poorhouse? … and on the upside, let's say I'm right, which I know I am…" [P1]
- Then re-ask with the tonality chain.

### F7. Raise the pain threshold
- Pain found in discovery is "held in harness" — "we don't solve it too quickly." People in pain act. Amplify the true cost of staying where they are, then future-pace the gap closed. [Y2, IG caption]

### F8. Price: "too expensive"
- "The moment you start defending the price you're arguing with their feelings." Agree ("You're right, it's premium") → create a fork: value issue (loop, build certainty) vs. budget issue (different situation). "Never defend the price. Redirect." [S03]

### F9. "Let me think about it"
- "Easiest objection in the book." Don't argue about what they need to think about. Ask whether they like the idea → find which ten is missing (product / you / company) → loop → close. [S05, IG caption]

## I. Selling Live (P1) — annotated flow (Belfort as a merchant banker selling a family organic-dairy operator)
1. Frame + permission to ask: "I want to know about you, your company, what's right, what's wrong."
2. Single pain question: "If you had to say one thing… giving you the most problem?" → China uncertainty.
3. Deepen + map authority/stakeholders (brothers, father, CFO).
4. Insert expertise as a question ("let me ask you a question… I was doing research…").
5. Offer value without asking (introductions across the supply chain).
6. Small-start close: "Give me 30 days… start small… sound fair enough?"
7. Objection ("we're a simple business… too complex") → "I hear what you're saying" → reframe ("all big companies were once medium-sized… I want to be the guy that takes you from 50 to 500").
8. Objection ("I don't know anything about your company… farming is built on trust") → hypothetical history reframe → reintroduce self (ten #2) → company longevity (ten #3).
9. Action threshold: worst case vs. upside.
10. Objection ("who else will I deal with?") → "only me".
11. Final chain: "I'm not asking to get married… one shot, 30 days, believe me you'll be a client for life — sound fair enough?" → "It sounds reasonable." → "Perfect, let's open up the account now."
Note how often he says "let me ask you a question" — that phrase is his control lever.
