# Evolv Closer Brain

This skill turns six sales experts into one way of running and reviewing an Evolv call. Each expert owns the part of the call they're best at. This file is the conductor: it says who leads at each moment, how to settle conflicts between them, and how to score calls the same way every time so 120 reviews can be compared.

**Evolv specifics** (products, pricing, offers, approved claims, ideal customer, the closer's own call data) come from the Project knowledge, not from this skill. Always pull them from there. If something Evolv-specific is missing (e.g. the price or the guarantee terms), ask instead of inventing it.

## 2. The Evolv call flow

Run every call in this order. The order matters because each stage builds the "juice" (Owen's term for credibility, proof and value) that pays for the next ask. Most objections are a sign that an earlier stage was skipped.

1. **Open (0–60s). Belfort + Miner + Daniel G.** Sound sharp, enthusiastic and expert straight away. No "how are you?". Set the agenda in a downplayed way, and sell the exit: *"The first part's pretty basic. I just want to learn about you and what's going on. If it's not a fit I'll tell you, and if it is, we can look at possible next steps. Would that help you?"* This takes away the pressure that causes the prospect to go guarded.
2. **Discovery. Miner.** Situation, then problem-awareness questions (find 3–5 problems, never sell to the first one), then the probing loop (repeat their emotion word, ask for an example, ask how long, ask the personal impact). Then solution-awareness (what they want, in their words), then consequence questions and why now, in a concerned tone. Hormozi's addition: put a number on the gap wherever you can. Aim for the prospect talking 80% of the time. Their reasons to buy must be in their own words, because people trust what they say themselves.
3. **Bridge and pre-handle. Hormozi + Voss + Daniel G.** Before any price, check the blockers: who else decides, time, money ranges, and what's stopped them before. Run a short accusation audit naming the likely negatives (*"You've probably tried things that didn't work, and this might sound like another one"*). Confirm with *"Why haven't you already solved this?"* Blockers raised before the price are easy to deal with. After the price, the same blockers become objections.
4. **Offer. Hormozi + Daniel G.** Spend 3 minutes or less presenting the offer as their game plan: where they are, where they said they want to be, and the 1-2-3 steps. Cover all four parts of the value equation: the outcome, proof it works, how fast the first win comes, and how little effort it takes them. Proof over promise: no "best", "amazing" or "perfect".
5. **Price. Hormozi + Daniel G.** Brace for it ("Okay, it is expensive"). Anchor on the full price, tie it to the gap they quantified, then go silent. If needed, break the price down to a cost per day. Never cut the price. Only change the terms, one step at a time: discount for paying upfront, then financing, then half down, then a payment plan.
6. **The ask. Daniel G + Belfort.** *"Would you be against getting started on this today?"* Say it with absolute certainty, then utter sincerity, then the reasonable man tone ("sound fair?"). Then stop talking.
7. **Objections. Voss first, then Daniel G.** Label what they're feeling, go silent, summarise until you hear "that's right", then ask a calibrated how/what question. Then make the attempt: lamb tone, offer an exit ("don't even worry about it"), find the real objection, and attempt again. Make at least 3 attempts on the call. Every attempt must bring something new (a label, a new piece of proof, a smaller first step); never repeat the same pitch louder. For money objections, use Hormozi's money frames. For "I'm not sure", use Belfort's approach: find which of the three tens (product, you, company) is weak and loop back on that one.
8. **Lock-in. Daniel G + Owen.** Take payment on the call ("certainty has a half-life"). Congratulate them, don't thank them. Then future-pace their first win and set clear next steps.

If they don't close: book a specific next call (not "I'll follow up"), and use Voss's one-line text if they go quiet: *"Have you given up on <their goal>?"* Don't try to persuade by text.

## 3. When the experts disagree

- **Energy: detached or fired up?** It depends on the stage. Use Miner's curious, detached tone in discovery (pressure makes people guard). Use Belfort's and Daniel G's certainty at the offer, price and ask (people buy certainty). Use Voss's calm late-night DJ voice the moment there's pushback (calm lowers defensiveness).
- **Persistence: 3+ attempts (Daniel G) or patience (Voss)?** Do both. Voss supplies how each attempt sounds; Daniel G supplies the number of attempts. An attempt that doesn't first label the prospect's feeling will feel like pressure.
- **Yes-questions or no-questions?** Prefer no-oriented questions for asks ("Would you be against…", "Is it a ridiculous idea…"). Saying "no" feels safe, so the prospect answers more honestly.
- **Who talks?** In discovery, the prospect. In the offer, the rep, briefly. After any ask, nobody: whoever speaks first after an ask usually loses.

## 4. Evolv guardrails (peptides)

Peptide sales are a place where one careless line can cost a refund, a chargeback or a regulatory problem. It also costs trust, and trust is the "juice" every method here depends on.
- Make only the product claims approved in the Evolv Project knowledge. Never say a product treats, cures or prevents a disease, and never promise a specific health or body result.
- Guarantees (Hormozi) cover terms, service and process, never health outcomes.
- Medical questions (medications, conditions, pregnancy, dosing for a condition): acknowledge the question, recommend they check with their doctor, and keep going. That's a credibility builder, not a lost sale.
- No fake scarcity or made-up social proof (Owen's WHITE-LIE). Use only real numbers and real deadlines.

In reviews, flag any breach with the tag `CLAIM-RISK`, even if the call closed.

## 5. How to use this skill

**Prep a call** (given lead notes or a booked call): give a one-screen plan:
- likely pain and goal
- 5 discovery questions tailored to this lead
- the blockers to pre-handle
- an offer outline (game plan in their terms)
- how to say the price, the ask in one line, and the 2 most likely objections with Voss and Daniel G responses
- one focus point taken from the closer's recent reviews, if any are in the Project

**Live, next line** (given a snippet or "they just said X"): first name the stage you're in. Then give the exact next line to say, in the closer's voice (one, maybe a backup), say why in one sentence, and name which expert it comes from. Keep it short, because the closer may be on the call.

**Objection help:** what the objection probably really means, then a label, then a calibrated question, then the re-attempt line. Name what earlier stage would have prevented it.

**Single call review:** use the master scorecard (section 6) and the single-call format. Pull rewrites from the expert who owns the weakest stage.

**Batch review** (many calls): section 7.

## 6. Master scorecard

Score each criterion 1–5. A 1 means absent or harmful, 3 means present but generic, 5 means the lead expert would hold it up as an example. For the anchors behind a 1, 3 and 5, see the lead expert's scorecard in their reference file.

| Code | Criterion | Lead expert | A 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| OPN | Open & frame | Belfort / Miner | Sharp and expert in the first seconds; downplayed agenda; the exit is sold; the prospect relaxes |
| DSC | Discovery depth | Miner | 3+ problems, each probed (example, how long, impact); the gap is put in numbers; the prospect talks about 80% of the time |
| URG | Urgency & why now | Miner | The prospect states the cost of waiting and why now, in their own words |
| PRE | Obstacles pre-handled | Hormozi / Voss | Decision-maker, time and money checked before the price; accusation audit done |
| OFR | Offer & value | Hormozi / Daniel G | 3 minutes or less, as a game plan in their terms; all 4 parts of the value equation; proof, not adjectives |
| PRC | Price delivery | Hormozi | Braced, anchored, tied to the gap, then silence; terms step down one at a time, never a price cut |
| ASK | The ask | Daniel G | A clear "would you be against…" ask, then silence |
| OBJ | Objection handling | Voss / Daniel G | Labels, "that's right", real objection found, 3+ attempts that each add something new |
| TON | Tone & control | Belfort | The right tone for each stage; the rep controls the frame; certain, never needy |
| INT | Integrity & compliance | Owen / Evolv | Proof over promise, no white lies, no CLAIM-RISK |

**Total out of 50.** Grades: A = 45 or more, B = 38–44, C = 30–37, D = 22–29, F = under 22. A CLAIM-RISK caps INT at 1.

**Leak tags.** Use only these, so the counts add up across calls:
- Owen's tags: HOW-ARE-YOU, NO-CONTEXT, VAGUE-PAIN, NO-DREAM, ANSWERED-NOT-ASKED, STEAL-SPACE, OBSCURE-NUMBER, PUSHED-CONCLUSION, WHITE-LIE, KNOCK-COMPETITION, NO-CHECK, NO-PREFRAME, PRICE-APOLOGY, EARLY-TIEDOWN, NO-ASK, HIDDEN-OBJ, SOFT-AFTER-OBJ, THANK-AT-CLOSE, TEXT-SAVE. They're defined in Owen's reference file.
- Added for this system:

| Tag | What it flags | Expert |
|---|---|---|
| SHALLOW-PROBE | Sold to the first problem, didn't probe | Miner |
| REP-TOLD-CONSEQUENCE | The rep, not the prospect, said the cost of waiting | Miner |
| POST-PRICE-OBSTACLE | A blocker that could have been checked surfaced only after the price | Hormozi |
| FEATURE-DUMP | Offer longer than about 5 minutes, or about features rather than their plan | Hormozi |
| PRICE-CUT | Lowered the price instead of changing the terms | Hormozi |
| ONE-ATTEMPT | Gave up or booked a follow-up after 0–1 attempts | Daniel G |
| NO-LABEL | Rebutted an objection without first naming the feeling | Voss |
| YES-STACK | Chained "makes sense, right?"-type tie-downs | Voss |
| LOST-FRAME | The prospect took over the agenda and the rep never brought it back | Belfort |
| FLAT-TONE | Monotone or hype, with the tone not matched to the stage | Belfort |
| CLAIM-RISK | A health or medical claim outside the approved claims | Evolv |

Strengths use Owen's positive labels: PAIN+DREAM, PROOF, DETACHED, PLAYFUL, SETUP-QUESTION, EXTRAPOLATE, REDUCE, PREFRAME, HIDDEN-OBJ-FOUND, PEACE-CLOSE, IDENTITY-CLOSE, UNEXPECTED-VALUE, REFERENCED-THEM.

## 7. Batch review (e.g. the last 3 weeks, about 120 calls)

Follow Owen's batch method (`references/experts/owen-santos-call-review.md`, section 9), using the master scorecard and tags above.

1. **Set up:** list the calls (ID, date, outcome: Closed / Not closed / Follow-up / Unknown; mark it "(inferred)" if you had to guess). Mark calls that could never have closed as "unqualified" (wrong person, no means at all, not a real lead) and keep them out of the win/loss comparison. That separates lead quality from skill.
2. **Score in chunks of about 10 calls**, one compact block per call:
```
#<n> <call id> | <date> | <outcome> | <length>
OPN_ DSC_ URG_ PRE_ OFR_ PRC_ ASK_ OBJ_ TON_ INT_ = __/50 (<grade>)
Leaks: <TAG>, <TAG>, <TAG>  (max 3, worst first)
Worst: "<quote>" [mm:ss] <TAG>
Best:  "<quote>" [mm:ss] <LABEL>
Fix:   "<rewrite of worst line>"
Lost at: <stage + TAG>  (non-closes only)
```
   After each chunk, update a running ledger (tag counts, average score per criterion, closed vs not closed) and also output the chunk as CSV rows:
   `call_id,date,outcome,OPN,DSC,URG,PRE,OFR,PRC,ASK,OBJ,TON,INT,total,tag1,tag2,tag3`
   The ledger and CSV let a long batch carry on in a new chat if it gets cut off, and let the closer paste the results into a spreadsheet. A single conversation can't hold 120 full transcripts, so expect to run about 10–20 calls per chat, save the ledger, and continue.
3. **Roll up** once all calls are scored:
   1. **Snapshot:** number of calls, close rate, average score, grade spread, average for each criterion (mark the lowest).
   2. **Top 3 recurring leaks:** count, % of calls, 2 verbatim examples with call IDs, why it costs deals (which expert says so), and the line to say instead.
   3. **Win vs loss:** a table of criterion averages for closed vs not-closed calls. The 2 criteria with the biggest gap are this closer's close predictors. Also note the stage where most losses happen.
   4. **Objection map:** each objection, what it really meant, how often it came up, how often it was saved, and the best save line, taken from the closer's own calls.
   5. **The closer's swipe file:** the 5 best moments (their own lines that worked) and the 5 worst moments, each with the rewrite.
   6. **Two-week drill plan.** One leak at a time; each block uses the drill from the expert who owns that leak:

      | Days | Focus |
      |---|---|
      | 1–4 | Leak #1 |
      | 5–8 | Leak #2 |
      | 9–11 | Leak #3 |
      | 12–13 | Full-call role-plays chaining all three fixes |
      | 14 | Re-review 10 new calls and compare against this batch |

   7. **One-line verdict:** the single change most likely to raise the close rate.

**Batch rules:** quote verbatim (if the transcript is garbled, say so). A pattern needs at least 3 calls or 10% of the batch, whichever is bigger; anything less is an anecdote. Use the same rubric for every call; spot-check early calls against late ones so the scoring doesn't get easier as the batch goes on. Rank by money impact: a leak that shows up in lost calls matters more than one that shows up in closed calls.
