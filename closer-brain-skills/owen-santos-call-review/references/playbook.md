# Owen Santos Call Review — Playbook

Deeper detail behind SKILL.md. Grouped by call situation. Each section: the principle, how Owen grades it, the reviewed call he taught it from, and word tracks.

---

## A. How Owen reviews a call (his review format)

What he does on every breakdown reel:
1. **Sets a challenge up front.** "He does three things in this video that cost you money not doing. See if you can name all three." / "Three things he did exceptionally well, one thing he messed up. Can you name all four?" / "Six high-level tactics here, but he misses on one."
2. **Stops after each meaningful line and labels it.** Vocabulary, roughly from best to worst:
   - "Super strong" / "Masterclass" / "So high level" — rare skill executed well
   - "Strong" — correct move
   - "Mid" — not wrong, not working (e.g., "Rapid two questions with no context: mid"; "Using an obscure number like a million is not believable: mid")
   - "Miss" / "Not ideal" / "Not great" — costs the deal or credibility
   - Occasional letter grade for a single moment: "A-minus opener."
3. **Gives the reason in one sentence**, always tied to a principle (juice, pain/dream, detachment, proof over promise, clarity, honesty).
4. **Gives the better line** when it's a miss ("Had he said, 'I have 10 other calls to make and 8 of them have already committed,' that hits different").
5. **Names what was missing at a resistance point** ("Her hesitancy answering these questions is because she doesn't understand yet [why]").
6. **Recaps the 3–4 big lessons** at the end and asks the viewer: "Can you do this with your product?"
7. **Separates preference from principle.** "Beware of any coach looking to package their preferences as principles." Flag clearly when a note is a preference (style) vs a principle (costs deals).
8. **Holds the line on ethics.** He reviews movie scams (Wolf of Wall Street, Boiler Room) for technique but marks lies as lies: "Both are unethically used, but you can do them ethically."

Coaching after the call (from his client stories):
- Diagnose the real meaning behind what the client said before fixing the rep's words (e.g., "can't afford it" = "I forgot why I bought").
- Give one concrete next action (e.g., "Always call and do not text").
- Then one skill to practice — the Mamba way — and measure it in the next calls.
- Celebrate the save/win publicly with the rep's own result.

## B. Juice vs squeeze (master frame for diagnosing objections)

> "All you're doing with things like this is building juice. If you have more juice than the squeeze required, which is to get them to say yes and give you money, you'll have a laydown with no objections." — Owen

- **Squeezes** on a typical call: stay on the line → answer personal questions → reveal numbers → accept a recommendation → hear price → say yes → pay/sign today.
- **Four core juice builders** (of "about 15"): credibility, scarcity, social proof, detachment. Plus unexpected value ("unexpected value, always juicing the sale") and humor/playfulness (buys time).
- **Review method:** for every objection or resistance moment, write: *squeeze being asked* → *juice present* → *juice missing*. The fix is usually earlier in the call.
- Ethical versions: real social proof with specifics ("We just did Max's house — red door, three down"); real scarcity ("I've got about an hour of downtime after Max's"); credibility from results/evidence, never fabricated.
- Make juice believable: concrete small numbers beat huge vague ones ("10 calls, 8 committed" > "a million doctors").

## C. Openers (cold, warm, booked)

Graded elements:
- **First name + familiar tone** as if they've spoken before ("super strong"). Offbeat pace that draws them in.
- **"How are you?"** on a cold call = miss: "Nobody believes from a cold call that you actually care about how they are." Also "Hey, how's it going" at a door = "very bad opener."
- **Confirm identity with a question:** "Is this Donnie?" gets more positive responses than "This is Donnie, right?"
- **Right into a question** = strong ("It looked like you were trying to sell a property over on 33rd. Is that still the case?").
- **Pattern interrupts:** "Do you want the good news or the bad news?" → "Bad news, this is a cold call. Good news, it's a well-researched cold call, and if you give me 20 seconds I can explain how I can help you." Owen: "Transparent and professional. With a time of 20 seconds he lowers the perceived effort."
- **Specificity proves research:** say the business name, not "this coffee shop."
- **Context before questions:** "Writing two questions with no context: mid." Prospect turns to "Are you trying to sell me something? I'm not interested" because no context was given.
- **Playful titles:** "Mr. Donnie" — playful, strong.
- **Self-deprecation** lowers guard: "Maybe my math is incorrect here, I dropped out of college" — "always a great move."
- **Gatekeepers are people too** with their own pain/dream — position the transfer as making them look good to their boss.

Gatekeeper word track (adapted from Owen):
> "Susie, typically when I talk to someone who isn't the owner, the first thing they want to do is hang up on me — I'm surprised you haven't. What I do saves owners like yours over $10,000 a month. When you put him in touch with someone who helps him keep $120,000 this year, what do you think that does for your relationship with him?" *(sit in the silence)* "...I'm a professional; the last thing I'll do is waste his time. The only reason you'd put me through is the chance I put six figures in his pocket this year. I'll make you look good. Is that fair?"

## D. Pain & dream discovery

- "Everybody has a pain & dream around what you sell. Even Eskimos."
- **The one-question move:** "What's the hardest part of your day?" — reveals what you're actually selling (not ice: sleep, health, longevity).
- **Extrapolate the dream:** 1 hour/day → 365 hours/year → quality and length of life. Then the reverse-sell: "If I offered you $10 a day to take that sleep back, would you sell me your health? ... Well, it's not $10, it's $8. Does this make perfect sense for you?"
- **Pain + dream in one sentence** ("Let's get you on to the next stage of your life — and get these tenants out of your ear").
- **Roadblock framing:** show the prospect's dream, name the roadblock between them and it, present the product as the vehicle over it.
- **Displace pain onto who they love most:** the scorpion was scary for the wife, not the husband — "he connected the pain to the person the prospect cares about the most."
- **Questions surface pain + social proof:** "Where are you from?" → "People from here are concerned about X" → newcomer feels it.
- **Answer questions with questions** to make them articulate their pain: Clooney's "What do you have against him?" "gives Ruben a stage to articulate his own pain."
- **Reference what they said** as the reason for your move — "shows he was listening, which is rare."
- **Small-business owner dream** is almost always "do more of the thing the business does, less sales and marketing."
- **Name a dream bigger than the product:** Don Draper renames "the wheel" to "the carousel" — "took the impact from a 6 or 7 to a 10... closed them without even asking for the sale."

## E. Presentation: proof over promise, detachment, stealing the space

- **Stealing the space** = "the common law that is broken across all of these calls." Saying "I'm the best web designer in town" — "not great." "Show versus tell. Proof over promise."
- **Elon test:** "Well, it worked." "This goes fast." — detached down-selling lets the audience get excited themselves. When he said "it can corner incredibly well," it didn't land — adjectives instead of evidence.
- **Detached phrasing ladder** (weak → strong): "This is the perfect call for you" → "This might be the perfect call for you" → "This may be a good call for you / may be helpful for you" (lets them place the adjective). "It'll probably benefit your business" beats "you need a website."
- **Lead with value, minimize effort:** "I already made you the website. All I need is five minutes. If you don't like it, you pay nothing." — "When value is very big and effort is very small, you tend to take a prospect and make them a client."
- **Results-based questions you already know the answer to** instead of knocking the competitor: "How's he doing for you? Make any money last year?" → offer proof ("I'll send you my earnings report right now").
- **Get them moving physically** (door-to-door: step outside to look) — "once he's moving in the way that you want, you have control."
- **Rapport without drift:** "Notice he's not off in left field talking about the cat."
- **Unexpected value:** offering movers for the seller's daughter.

## F. Clarity: questions & transitions

Owen's six questions (full reasoning):
1. **"What questions do you still have?"** — beats "Does that make sense?" Use at every transition from step to step so they stay clear. "Confused people do not buy, clear people do. My job as a closer is to move you to clarity."
2. **"What do you hate about this?" → "What do you love?"** — hate extracts the objection "like a syringe"; love lets them sell themselves; ask follow-ups on what they love.
3. **"Outside of the investment, is there anything still giving you hesitation?"** — price is often just "the easiest thing to blame." If no → price is the only thing left to solve.
4. **"What would need to happen for you to feel 100% comfortable moving forward today?"** — "'I'm not ready' is not a real objection. It's incomplete information at best. Ready for what?" "Persuasion does not occur in the vague."
5. **"What's the rich man's cost here?"** — intriguing; the rich man's cost is the cost of inaction. "The poor man talks about the cost of buying. The rich man talks about the cost of waiting." Raises your status while you explain it.
6. **"What haven't we talked through yet that decides things for you?"** — "The objection you don't uncover is always the one that costs you in the end."

Other clarity moves:
- **"Get clients specific."** "Where clients get in trouble is when they stay vague about what's going to happen to the person they care about most." E.g., "Is four years definitely enough time for Sally, or does she need more?"
- **Answer-with-a-question to set up the close:** asked "What would you charge?" → "Have you shopped around? What did they quote you?" → "$1,000" → "Would you be concerned if I only charged $500?" — prospect calls it a good price before seeing the product. Owen: "Best part of the whole call."

## G. Price & numbers

- **Pre-frame, don't drop:** "I'd have to be quite a bit lower for this to make sense" + expertise ("software that tells me what properties sell for once fixed up") — "pre-framing as opposed to just dropping it on her."
- **Playful at price:** "In a perfect world I'd be around 150 — oh, don't hang up on me, Maria." "The playfulness, even while talking about price, is so rare."
- **Extrapolate to the extreme (value)**: $5,000/month missed → "$60,000 a year — you're paying a salary by missing 10 calls." "99% of salespeople miss" this.
- **Reduce to the ridiculous (cost)**: $30/month → $1/day; "less than a cup of coffee a day."
- **Specific cost of inaction:** reviewing a hedge-fund pitch that said "the growth you'll have missed out on," Owen flags "growth is some vague idea... not painful enough." Fix: "$10M over 15 years at a normal return becomes ~$31M. With me it's [more]. Feel how that lands different?"
- **Options + smallest comfortable start:** "Does it make sense to start with the smallest amount you're comfortable with so you aren't over-committing, but you're no longer leaving your family at risk?" / "Option 1 pays the whole mortgage, option 2 buys Sally four years — which makes the most sense for the budget while still giving her the protection she deserves?"
- **Budget check:** "Is $159 going to take any food off the table? Not putting you on the ramen-noodle diet?"
- **Transparent pricing** is praised ("clean, transparent pricing").

## H. Closing & "I want to think about it"

Owen's thesis: the process should pre-handle objections; when they come anyway, have trained responses. He wrote 15, then 25, rebuttals for "think about it" alone.

1. **Days or weeks + three questions:**
   > "Awesome. Which do you want, two or three days or two or three weeks?" — "Two or three days." — "Cool. Funny thing, I'm a consumer too. I held off on big moves until the pain of staying the same got bigger than the pain of change. A wealthy mentor taught me that no matter how long I think, I'm asking the same three questions. Mind if I share them? One: is change good — does it solve the problem and fit the budget? [confirm] Two: is it reasonable that [company], who has helped hundreds of thousands of people, can do the same for you? [confirm] Three: am I the kind of person you want helping you with this? [confirm] ... Final question: will having this in place bring you less peace or more peace?" *(wait — "they will always pick more peace")* "If all that's true, does it feel right to [submit/start] today and see if we can secure this for [loved one] the way you were hoping?"
2. **Overthinker loop:**
   > "Have you ever said 'I want to think about it' about anything and never acted, and missed out? ... Do you think that might be why [loved one] is still at risk? ... Might that be a reason to do something new this time?"
   Then "let's pump the brakes" → reasonable-to-believe question → options → budget check → more/less peace → "go ahead and finish the application."
3. **Two doors (risk transfer):**
   > "Thinking about it is definitely the right move, so let's be crystal clear so you stay in control. Door A: we leave this unfinished — if something happens, who carries the risk, your wife or the company? Door B: we finish the option that doesn't hurt your budget and you move the risk off her tonight. We usually think things over to avoid risk — so which door is actually the risky one?"
4. **Six weeks from now (not ready now):**
   > "Imagine six weeks from now, you still haven't done this — because that's what people do when they don't do it on the call — and then something happens. If you could come back to this moment, what would you tell yourself to do?" → "That's why educated [homeowners] who understand this the way you and I do now get it done while they still can. We don't know God's plan... these windows close every day. Does it make sense to start with the smallest amount you're comfortable with?"
5. **Identity close:** "The only question you'll have to ask your wife is what kind of hardwood floors she wants in her new house." Owen: the statement is really a question — "are you the hero you've always told yourself you are?"
6. **Detachment close:** "In a year's time you won't be able to get me on the phone." / "Sorry we bothered you" and actually leaving — curiosity follows detachment.
7. **After yes:** Don't thank them ("Why would you thank someone you did a favor for?"). Lock details immediately.

Saving cancellations (post-sale):
- "Always call and do not text. Persuasion doesn't occur via text."
- Filter what they say: "can't afford it / don't want it / found something different" → "I forgot why I bought this. Please help me reconnect with that reason."
- Reconnect the why; tell a story of someone who did what they're about to do and the pain that followed; offer a reduced option.

## I. Scheduling and brush-offs

- Booking "tomorrow at 7" when today was possible = miss: "Scheduling appointments for the next day, never recommended if it can be avoided."
- "Next week" = brush-off ("she's not going to show up"). Redirect: "Do you want next month or next year? Whether we do this now or later, the money keeps getting left on the table. Got 15 minutes this evening so we can make your business more money and you can get back to more important things?" Tie appointment to dream outcome, not to the meeting.

## J. Credibility leaks and integrity

- "I'm going to be very honest" / "Can I be honest with you?" / "Gotta be honest isn't my favorite thing to say" — "implies you haven't been honest before."
- "I hate to play devil's advocate" — "An opinion from a salesperson is advice from a salesperson, and who wants advice from a salesperson?"
- "This will absolutely change your life" → ask "Do you believe it will change yours?" ("because they found their own way there").
- "Why is this a right-now thing for you?" — only as a tie-down after soft commits; otherwise sparks sales resistance.
- "Do you mind if I coach you for a minute?" — only when selling coaching and you've clearly positioned above their status.
- Insulting the competition — "usually a weak move"; only works with huge status.
- White lies — "eats away your integrity over enough time and you will start to resent what you do."
- Cutting corners — the high performer who never keeps the big checks "uses his high performance [to be] lazy when it comes to the details... the way and the reason that you do things matters more than the initial outcome."
- Owner vs rep caveat: "When you sell as the owner of the product you can get away with things non-owners cannot." Grade reps by rep standards.

## K. Tone & delivery details

- Familiar, welcoming tone; a regional warmth (he singles out a Southern accent as highly trusted in the US).
- Offbeat pace; not script cadence.
- Concerned tone on the pattern-break question lowers guard — "can't sell anything while [it's a fight]."
- Calm voice that doesn't ask permission (his note on the Milgram obedience study: "People don't fold under pressure. They fold for a calm voice that doesn't ask for permission") — with the warning that the sale only sticks if you connected them to a real reason to have the product.
- Playful throughout, especially at price; humor + landing the laugh.
- High energy and ease: on cold calls, doing "with ease" what 99.99% find terrifying is attractive.
- Conviction at the pivotal moment, fed by "drowning yourself in proof" the product works.
- Confident, courageous tone sets up the big identity-defining statement.

## L. Practice & coaching the rep

- **Mamba way:** Kobe did basic fundamentals drills and "wouldn't stop working on that one thing until he had actually gotten better." Translate: "Identify the part of your sales process you're weakest at and do nothing but work on that thing for one hour." Owen: top-3 most impactful thing you can do; "practice is vague... mastery of any sales skill in under 20 hours" when practice is specific.
- **Steal from others' calls:** take unsolicited sales calls; write down the one sentence that made you consider it; use it in your process. "Being a student while resisting being sold is harder than it sounds."
- **Notebook, not phone** for the gold from calls/trainings; implementation over learning ("I'd rather read one great book and implement 100% of it").
- **Identity as multiplier** (Pinnacle training): Results = effort × skills × identity. "Identity is the ability to act based on who you are instead of how you feel." Coaching question for a rep: "What does the version of you that closes at [target]% do on this call?" Daily accountability: "If the person I care about most could see every moment of yesterday, would they be proud?"
- **Rejection reframe:** "Rejection no longer means death. It means growth. Embrace it, pursue it."

## M. Full leak-tag definitions (batch consistency)

| Tag | Stage | Definition (flag when...) |
|---|---|---|
| HOW-ARE-YOU | Open | Rep opens with "how are you / how's it going" before any reason to talk |
| NO-CONTEXT | Open | Rep asks ≥2 questions before stating why they're calling / why it matters |
| GENERIC | Open | Generic reference where a specific name/detail was available |
| VAGUE-PAIN | Discovery | Pain/cost left unquantified or abstract when a number was obtainable |
| NO-DREAM | Discovery | Desired outcome never stated in prospect's words |
| DRIFT | Discovery | >2 min rapport/tangent without return to purpose |
| ANSWERED-NOT-ASKED | Any | Rep answers a buying question flat instead of asking a setup question |
| STEAL-SPACE | Present | Positive adjective about self/product ("best," "amazing," "perfect") |
| OBSCURE-NUMBER | Present | Unbelievable or vague magnitude used as proof |
| PUSHED-CONCLUSION | Present/Close | Rep states the conclusion the prospect should reach |
| OPINION | Any | Rep offers personal opinion/advice ("I think you should," "devil's advocate") |
| HONESTLY | Any | "Honestly / to be honest / can I be honest / gotta be honest" |
| WHITE-LIE | Any | Fabricated scarcity, proof, or claim |
| KNOCK-COMPETITION | Any | Disparages current provider/alternative |
| NO-CHECK | Transitions | Moves stages without a clarity check, or uses "does that make sense?" |
| NO-PREFRAME | Price | Price stated with no pre-frame or value tie |
| PRICE-APOLOGY | Price | Hedging/apologizing for price ("I know it's a lot") |
| EARLY-TIEDOWN | Close | Urgency/tie-down before any soft commits |
| NO-ASK | Close | Call ends without a clear ask for the decision |
| HIDDEN-OBJ | Close | Stated objection accepted at face value; real one never surfaced |
| SOFT-AFTER-OBJ | Objection | Rep folds, argues, or over-explains after an objection |
| TOMORROW | Scheduling | Next step pushed out when same-day was possible; brush-off accepted |
| THANK-AT-CLOSE | Close | Rep thanks the prospect for buying |
| NEEDY | Tone | Chasing, pleading, over-talking, no willingness to walk |
| FLAT | Tone | Monotone/scripted cadence; no warmth or play |
| TEXT-SAVE | Post-call | Attempts persuasion/save via text instead of a call |

Positive labels for "Best" moments (use consistently): PAIN+DREAM, PROOF, DETACHED, PLAYFUL, SETUP-QUESTION, EXTRAPOLATE, REDUCE, PREFRAME, HIDDEN-OBJ-FOUND, PEACE-CLOSE, IDENTITY-CLOSE, UNEXPECTED-VALUE, REFERENCED-THEM.
